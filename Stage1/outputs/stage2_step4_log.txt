1. HOW LARGE IS EACH ASSOCIATION?
======================================================================
Q1 asks how large each association is across the observed range of the
variable. A p-value does not answer that and neither does an edf. Below,
each covariate is moved from its 10th to its 90th percentile with everything
else held fixed, and the change in predicted density is reported as a ratio.
Because the contrast is a difference of two rows of the same design matrix,
every term that does not involve the covariate cancels exactly — including

2. SPATIAL RESIDUAL CHECK
======================================================================
If the site random effect is doing its job, site-level mean residuals should
show no spatial structure. Sites within an island group sit a few hundred
metres apart, so this is tested WITHIN each region: across regions the only
thing a global test would detect is the 200 km gap between them.

      region n_sites Moran_I expected p_perm median_sep_km min_sep_km
1       Palm      30  0.0110  -0.0345 0.8778          5.43      0.444
2 Whitsunday      41  0.1457  -0.0250 0.0066          8.93      0.355

Inverse-distance weights, 4999 permutations. Under no spatial structure
Moran's I sits near -1/(n-1), which is slightly below zero, not at zero.

CAUTION — these are SINGLE-SEED values and must not be read as a result.
Randomised quantile residuals draw from a uniform, so Moran's I and its
permutation p-value both depend on set.seed(). Step 5 repeats this across
twenty seeds and supersedes the numbers above. Its finding: Whitsunday's I is
positive under every seed (0.079 to 0.167) while Palm's is near zero under
every seed, but the Whitsunday permutation p falls below 0.05 in only 11 of
20 randomisations. Residual spatial structure in Whitsunday is suggested,
not established. See coral_trout_stage2_step5.R and stage2_step5_log.txt.

5. WITHIN AND BETWEEN HABITAT EFFECTS (Q4)
======================================================================
A pooled habitat coefficient answers two questions at once and separates
neither: do sites with more structure hold more fish (between-site, and
confounded by everything else fixed about a site), and does a site that gains
structure gain fish (within-site, immune to any time-invariant site
characteristic). Q4 asks the second. Splitting each habitat covariate into a
site mean and a within-site deviation estimates both.

depth is site-constant in this subset (varies at 0 of 71 sites), so it has no
within-site component and is left unsplit.
