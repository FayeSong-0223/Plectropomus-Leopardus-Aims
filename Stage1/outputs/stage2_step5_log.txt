1. CONCURVITY
======================================================================
Concurvity measures how far one smooth can be reproduced by the others. It is
the GAM analogue of collinearity, it is not visible in a p-value, and it was
never computed for this model until now. Values approach 1 when a term is
nearly redundant. The 'worst' column is the pessimistic bound.

         para s(YEAR):REGIONPalm s(YEAR):REGIONWhitsunday s(rugosity) s(LHC)
worst       1              0.879                    0.914       0.644  0.750
observed    1              0.597                    0.602       0.609  0.446
estimate    1              0.481                    0.634       0.568  0.690
         s(kd490) s(maxDHW) s(Cyclone) s(SITE)
worst       0.926     0.951      0.655   1.000
observed    0.573     0.537      0.571   0.082
estimate    0.775     0.744      0.351   0.183

terms with worst-case concurvity above 0.8:
   s(SITE)  1.000
   s(maxDHW)  0.951
   s(kd490)  0.926
   s(YEAR):REGIONWhitsunday  0.914
   s(YEAR):REGIONPalm  0.879

This is the number behind a caveat stated qualitatively throughout: the thermal
and turbidity smooths are substantially reproducible from the region-specific
year trends, because both vary largely at region-year level. Their coefficients
should be read as one of several possible splits of shared variation, not as
independently identified effects.

2. BASIS DIMENSION (k.check)
======================================================================
k was fixed at 5 throughout with a stated justification. That justification was
never tested. A k-index below 1 with a small p-value means residual pattern
remains that the basis cannot represent.

                         k'     edf k-index p-value

s(SITE)                  71 36.3363      NA      NA

flagged: s(YEAR):REGIONPalm, s(YEAR):REGIONWhitsunday, s(kd490)

For the year smooths this is expected and not fixable by raising k: Palm has
only six distinct survey years, so k cannot exceed six, and genuine year-to-year
jumps are not smooth in any basis. Model B, which saturates time with region-year
as a factor, is the specification that answers this, and it agrees on protection.
For kd490 the flag is a real limitation and is reported as such.

======================================================================
4. DEPTH: WHY IT IS NOW A LINEAR TERM
======================================================================
depth varies at 0 of 71 sites in this subset. A penalised smooth of a
site-constant covariate is fully aliased with the site random effect, and the
concurvity check above returned exactly 1.000 for it before this change.

        specification    AIC dev_expl            W1987            W2004
1   depth as a smooth 2353.1    60.2% 3.21 (2.25-4.58) 2.89 (1.97-4.24)
2 depth linear (used) 2353.0    60.2% 3.21 (2.25-4.58) 2.89 (1.97-4.23)
3       depth omitted 2354.1    59.3% 3.62 (2.59-5.04) 3.34 (2.36-4.72)

The smooth and the linear term give identical estimates, because the smooth had
already collapsed to a straight line (edf 1.005). Omitting depth raises the
Whitsunday estimate, so depth is doing real adjustment work and should be kept.
The linear form keeps that adjustment without the aliasing.

5. ZERO COUNTS AGAINST WHAT THE MODEL PREDICTS
======================================================================
Section 8 of the project plan flagged that Palm's zeros are concentrated, and
no distributional check was ever run against that. The negative binomial can
accommodate a good deal of excess zero mass through its overdispersion, but
whether it accommodates THIS much is a question with an answer.

The test is a parametric bootstrap. Each replicate draws a new dataset from the
fitted model — same covariates, same fitted means, same theta — counts its
zeros, and the observed count is compared against that reference distribution.
An analytic expected value alone would not do: it gives a point to compare
against with no sense of how far a correct model would ordinarily stray.

observed zeros: 52 of 467 observations
simulated under the fitted model: median 35, 95% interval 25 to 46
one-sided p (simulated >= observed): 0.0040
-> the model generates fewer zeros than observed. Excess zero mass is real.

The same comparison across the low counts, where zero inflation would show up
as a zero excess paired with a deficit at one and two:

  count observed sim_median lo     hi
1     0       52         35 27 47.000
2     1       47         56 44 69.000
3     2       57         59 46 73.525
4     3       38         53 41 67.000
5     4       42         45 34 57.000
6     5       27         37 26 47.000

By region, since the plan's concern was specifically about Palm:
      region   n observed sim_median     lo hi      p
1       Palm 180       31         20 12.975 28 0.0090
2 Whitsunday 287       21         15  8.000 22 0.0619

What this changes. A zero excess inflates the apparent overdispersion, which
widens intervals rather than narrowing them, so it does not manufacture the

sites where at least 4 of 7 surveys are zero: 1 (holding 8% of all zeros)

6. TEMPORAL AUTOCORRELATION WITHIN SITES
======================================================================
The model gives each site a random intercept, which absorbs a site's persistent
level. It does not model correlation between successive visits to the same site
beyond that, and nothing in this project had tested whether it needs to.

Survey years are unequally spaced — 2007, 2009, 2012, 2014, 2016, 2017, 2018 —
so pairing consecutive SURVEYS would mix a one-year gap with a three-year gap
and call both 'lag 1'. Pairs are therefore binned by the actual number of years
between them.

   gap_years n_pairs      r     lo     hi      p
1          1      82  0.046 -0.173  0.260 0.6845
2          2     284 -0.028 -0.144  0.088 0.6325
3          3     112 -0.376 -0.525 -0.205 0.0000
4          4     142 -0.090 -0.251  0.075 0.2844
5          5     183 -0.085 -0.227  0.061 0.2519
6          6      71 -0.194 -0.409  0.041 0.1047
7          7     142 -0.128 -0.287  0.037 0.1277
8          8      41  0.069 -0.244  0.369 0.6694
9          9     142 -0.028 -0.192  0.137 0.7399
10        10      41  0.126 -0.189  0.418 0.4309
11        11      71 -0.087 -0.314  0.149 0.4706

One caution before reading that table. A site random intercept forces a site's
residuals to sum to roughly zero, which induces a negative correlation of about
-1/(m-1) at every gap, with m the surveys per site. Here m = 6.58, so about -0.179 is the baseline to judge
against, not zero. Most gaps sit at or above it. One does not.

Because randomised quantile residuals depend on the seed, the strongest gap —
3 years — is re-tested across 20 randomisations, as every other residual
diagnostic here is:

gap of 3 years: r ranges -0.399 to -0.326, median -0.367, p < 0.05 in 20 of 20 randomisations
-> this is not a seed artefact and it is stronger than the induced baseline.

Which survey pairs carry it:

                        n_sites      r      p
Palm 2009 to 2012            30 -0.397 0.0298
Whitsunday 2009 to 2012      41 -0.501 0.0008
Whitsunday 2014 to 2017      41 -0.228 0.1508

What it means. A negative correlation is a reversal: sites sitting above their
predicted density at the first survey sat below it at the second, and the other
way round. Something between those two surveys moved sites differentially, and
the model does not have a term for it. The region-specific year trend cannot
supply one — it shifts every site in a region by the same amount.

This is the cyclone limitation made concrete rather than merely stated. Cyclone
exposure is recorded at survey points only, so any disturbance falling in the
gap between two surveys is invisible to the covariate, and the gap concerned
here is a three-year one. Attributing it to a particular storm would be
inference beyond this dataset; what the data support is that the gap contains
an unmodelled, site-differentiating event. It belongs in the limitations and in
the list of questions for anyone who holds the track data.

7. MULTIPLICITY WITHIN HYPOTHESIS FAMILIES
======================================================================
Model A reports 17 tests. None of the p-values quoted anywhere in this project
had been adjusted for that, which matters most for the terms that are already
marginal.

A single correction across all 17 would be the wrong instrument. The plan set
out four hypotheses in advance, and a test only competes with the others asked
in service of the same hypothesis. Holm's correction is applied within each
family. It controls the family-wise error rate, needs no independence
assumption — which matters here, since concurvity between these terms is high —
and is uniformly at least as powerful as Bonferroni.

              family              term      p p_holm survives
1   H1/Q3 management          NTR 1987 0.2099 0.4198    FALSE
2   H1/Q3 management          NTR 2004 0.8629 0.8629    FALSE
3   H1/Q3 management REGION x NTR 1987 0.0004 0.0014     TRUE
4   H1/Q3 management REGION x NTR 2004 0.0007 0.0020     TRUE
5         H2 habitat       s(rugosity) 0.0971 0.2912    FALSE
6         H2 habitat            s(LHC) 0.3748 0.3748    FALSE
7         H2 habitat             depth 0.0991 0.2912    FALSE
8     H3 environment         s(maxDHW) 0.0000 0.0000     TRUE
9     H3 environment          s(kd490) 0.0136 0.0272     TRUE
10    H3 environment        s(Cyclone) 0.0776 0.0776    FALSE
11 H4 site variation           s(SITE) 0.0000 0.0000     TRUE

The result that matters survives comfortably. Q3 is the region-by-protection
interaction, and both its terms clear Holm within the management family by an
order of magnitude. The NTR main effects do not, but those are the Palm
estimates, which were already null — a null that fails a correction for
multiplicity is still a null.

Nothing in the habitat family survives, which agrees with the within-between
split in Step 4.

The environment family is where the two corrections part company. The thermal
term survives both Holm and the unconditional interval. Cyclone exposure
survives neither. Turbidity survives Holm but its magnitude interval includes
1 once smoothing-parameter uncertainty is admitted, so it should be reported as
unresolved rather than as an effect — the weaker of two readings is the one to
quote when they disagree.

The year-trend smooths are not in any family. They are adjustment terms rather
than hypotheses, and correcting a hypothesis test for the significance of a
nuisance term would be a category error.
