1. CONCURVITY
======================================================================
Concurvity measures how far one smooth can be reproduced by the others. It is
the GAM analogue of collinearity, it is not visible in a p-value, and it was
never computed for this model until now. Values approach 1 when a term is
nearly redundant. The 'worst' column is the pessimistic bound.

         para s(YEAR):REGIONPalm s(YEAR):REGIONWhitsunday s(rugosity) s(LHC)
worst       1              0.879                    0.914       0.644  0.750
observed    1              0.597                    0.602       0.609  0.446
estimate    1              0.481                    0.634       0.568  0.690
         s(kd490) s(maxDHW) s(Cyclone) s(SITE)
worst       0.926     0.951      0.655   1.000
observed    0.573     0.537      0.571   0.082
estimate    0.775     0.744      0.351   0.183

terms with worst-case concurvity above 0.8:
   s(SITE)  1.000
   s(maxDHW)  0.951
   s(kd490)  0.926
   s(YEAR):REGIONWhitsunday  0.914
   s(YEAR):REGIONPalm  0.879

This is the number behind a caveat stated qualitatively throughout: the thermal
and turbidity smooths are substantially reproducible from the region-specific
year trends, because both vary largely at region-year level. Their coefficients
should be read as one of several possible splits of shared variation, not as
independently identified effects.

Read the k-index and the protection estimate together. If raising k leaves the
flag in place, the residual pattern is not a basis-dimension problem and no k
will fix it; if the protection estimate does not move, the flag is not a threat
to the result whatever its cause.

year : k-index 0.862 at k = 5 and 0.863 at k = 6, the maximum the survey years allow -> unchanged

In both cases the residual pattern the check detects is not something a larger
basis can represent, and in both cases the protection estimate is unmoved. Model B
saturates time with region-year as a factor, which is the specification that
answers the year flag directly rather than by enlarging a basis; it agrees with
Model A on protection to within 0.02. The kd490 flag remains a real limitation on
what can be claimed about turbidity, and turbidity is reported as unresolved for
that reason among others.

4. DEPTH: WHY IT IS NOW A LINEAR TERM
======================================================================
depth varies at 0 of 71 sites in this subset. A penalised smooth of a
site-constant covariate is fully aliased with the site random effect, and the
concurvity check above returned exactly 1.000 for it before this change.

        specification    AIC dev_expl            W1987            W2004
1   depth as a smooth 2353.1    60.2% 3.21 (2.25-4.58) 2.89 (1.97-4.24)
2 depth linear (used) 2353.0    60.2% 3.21 (2.25-4.58) 2.89 (1.97-4.23)
3       depth omitted 2354.1    59.3% 3.62 (2.59-5.04) 3.34 (2.36-4.72)

The smooth and the linear term give identical estimates, because the smooth had
already collapsed to a straight line (edf 1.005). Omitting depth raises the
Whitsunday estimate, so depth is doing real adjustment work and should be kept.
The linear form keeps that adjustment without the aliasing.

5-6. TWO GOODNESS-OF-FIT QUESTIONS, ONE SIMULATION
======================================================================
Two things had never been tested: whether the negative binomial accounts for the
observed zeros, and whether a site random intercept is enough to account for
dependence between repeat visits to a site. Both are questions about whether data
generated by this model would look like the data in hand, so both are answered
from one parametric bootstrap rather than from two ad-hoc tests.

The procedure, per replicate: simulate a full dataset from the fitted model;
refit the same model to it; compute the test statistics from the refit exactly as
they are computed from the real fit. Refitting is the part that makes this a
parametric bootstrap rather than a conditional simulation — it carries the
estimation uncertainty that a fixed-mu simulation leaves out, and it means each
replicate is scored against a model fitted to it, as the observed data are.

This design also removes two flaws in the earlier version of these checks. The
residual correlation statistic is compared against its own simulated null rather
than against zero, so the negative correlation a site random intercept induces
mechanically is already in the reference distribution. And the search across
eleven survey gaps is handled by taking the most extreme gap as the statistic, so
the null distribution is the null distribution of that search — not of one gap
chosen after seeing the answers.

7. MULTIPLICITY WITHIN HYPOTHESIS FAMILIES
======================================================================
Model A reports 17 tests. None of the p-values quoted anywhere in this project
had been adjusted for that, which matters most for the terms that are already
marginal.

A single correction across all 17 would be the wrong instrument. The plan set
out four hypotheses in advance, and a test only competes with the others asked
in service of the same hypothesis. Holm's correction is applied within each
family. It controls the family-wise error rate, needs no independence
assumption — which matters here, since concurvity between these terms is high —
and is uniformly at least as powerful as Bonferroni.

              family              term       p  p_holm survives
1   H1/Q3 management          NTR 1987  0.2099  0.4198    FALSE
2   H1/Q3 management          NTR 2004  0.8629  0.8629    FALSE
3   H1/Q3 management REGION x NTR 1987  0.0004  0.0014     TRUE
4   H1/Q3 management REGION x NTR 2004  0.0007  0.0020     TRUE
5         H2 habitat       s(rugosity)  0.0971  0.2912    FALSE
6         H2 habitat            s(LHC)  0.3748  0.3748    FALSE
7         H2 habitat             depth  0.0991  0.2912    FALSE
8     H3 environment         s(maxDHW) <0.0001 <0.0001     TRUE
9     H3 environment          s(kd490)  0.0136  0.0272     TRUE
10    H3 environment        s(Cyclone)  0.0776  0.0776    FALSE
11 H4 site variation           s(SITE) <0.0001 <0.0001     TRUE

The result that matters survives comfortably. Q3 is the region-by-protection
interaction, and both its terms clear Holm within the management family by an
order of magnitude. The NTR main effects do not, but those are the Palm
estimates, which were already null — a null that fails a correction for
multiplicity is still a null.

Nothing in the habitat family survives, which agrees with the within-between
split in Step 4.

The environment family is where the two corrections part company. The thermal
term survives both Holm and the unconditional interval. Cyclone exposure
survives neither. Turbidity survives Holm but its magnitude interval includes
1 once smoothing-parameter uncertainty is admitted, so it should be reported as
unresolved rather than as an effect — the weaker of two readings is the one to
quote when they disagree.

The year-trend smooths are not in any family. They are adjustment terms rather
than hypotheses, and correcting a hypothesis test for the significance of a
nuisance term would be a category error.
