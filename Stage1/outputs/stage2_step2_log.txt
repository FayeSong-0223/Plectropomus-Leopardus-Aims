4. HOW MUCH DOES EACH BLOCK CONTRIBUTE?  (Q2)
======================================================================
Q2 asks how much of the variation the covariates explain. Two earlier answers
have been withdrawn, and the reasons matter more than the numbers did.

Withdrawn 1: a link-scale variance decomposition reporting management at 0.481.
  It was not invariant to the coding of the factor contrasts. Refitting the
  identical model under sum-to-zero moved management to 0.391 and space/time
  from 0.121 to 0.345, with identical fitted values and log-likelihood. It also
  partitioned only the systematic half of the variation, and used the shrunken
  random-effect predictions for the site block, understating it by about half.

Withdrawn 2: a drop-one-block comparison in which management was measured by
  removing it from a model with no site random effect. That number, 14.09
  percentage points, is not a management contribution. With no random effect
  present, the management terms absorb every between-site difference that
  happens to align with zoning — habitat, history, location, anything fixed
  about a site. It measures confounding as much as management, and reporting
  it as management's share would have repeated the error it was meant to fix.

What is reported instead is set out below. It begins with the thing that has to
be said plainly rather than measured.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
4a. WHAT IS NOT IDENTIFIABLE, AND WHY
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Protection changes within 0 of 71 sites.

That single fact settles it. Protection is a fixed property of a site across the
whole series, so its entire contribution is between-site. A site random intercept
is also, by construction, entirely between-site. The two are estimated from the
same 71 degrees of freedom, and no partition of variance or of deviance can say
how much of a between-site difference belongs to zoning rather than to whatever
else distinguishes those sites. Drop management and the random effect absorbs it;
drop the random effect and management absorbs everything else.

This is a limit of the design, not of the method, and it cannot be fixed by
choosing a better statistic. What CAN be estimated is the size of the protection
contrast, with an interval, which is Step 1 and Step 3's business — and how much
the model's ability to predict a site it has never seen depends on knowing that
site's zoning. That second question is answerable, and it is asked in 4c.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
4b. DROP-ONE-BLOCK AT FIXED DISPERSION
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Each block is removed in turn and the fall in deviance explained recorded.

One correction to how this was done before: nb() re-estimates theta for every
model, so a reduced model can absorb its lost structure into a smaller theta and
the two deviances are then measured on different scales, which makes the
difference uninterpretable. Every model below is fitted with theta held at the
full model's estimate, using negbin(), so all deviances share one scale.

Management is included for completeness and is expected to be near zero for the
reason given in 4a. It is not evidence of no effect.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
4c. OUT-OF-SAMPLE: WHAT DOES KNOWING A NEW SITE'S ZONING BUY?
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The in-sample comparisons above cannot separate management from site identity
because both are fitted to the same sites. Prediction to a site the model has
never seen removes that overlap: for an unseen site there is no random intercept
to estimate, so the fixed effects have to carry the prediction alone, and
management either helps or it does not.

Ten folds, split by SITE so that a site is never in both training and test.
Each model is fitted on the training sites and scored on the held-out sites by
negative binomial log predictive density, with the random effect set to zero for
unseen sites and each model using its own theta — a predictive comparison is
between complete distributions, so re-estimating theta is correct here.

This measures predictive contribution, not a share of variance, and it does not
license a causal reading: a zoning label may predict a new site well because of
what protection does, or because of what protection is correlated with.

Total log predictive density of the full model across all held-out sites: -1232.3

How to read this, and how not to.
  It is a predictive statement: knowing a new site's zoning improves the
  prediction of its counts by this much on the log scale. It does not partition
  variance, the entries do not sum to anything, and a large value is not evidence
  that management contributes more variance than site identity — that comparison
  is unavailable in this design by any route, as 4a sets out.
  It is not causal. Zoning may predict an unseen site well because of what
  protection does, or because of whatever the zoning process selected for. This
  analysis cannot tell those apart, and nothing here should be read as trying to.
