4. HOW MUCH DOES EACH BLOCK CONTRIBUTE?
======================================================================
Q2 asks how much of the variation the covariates explain. An earlier version of
this analysis answered it by decomposing the variance of the linear predictor
into per-block shares. That answer has been withdrawn, for three reasons found
on audit and recorded in DECISIONS.md:

  1. It was not invariant to the coding of the factor contrasts. Refitting the
     identical model under sum-to-zero rather than treatment contrasts moved the
     management share from 0.481 to 0.391 and space/time from 0.121 to 0.345,
     with identical fitted values and identical log-likelihood. A quantity that
     moves when nothing about the fit moves is a property of the parameterisation,
     not of the data.
  2. It partitioned only the systematic part. The linear predictor has variance
     0.533; the negative binomial observation process contributes roughly 0.455
     more on the same scale. Around half of the variation sat outside the
     partition and the shares were silently conditional on that.
  3. The site block used the empirical variance of the shrunken random-effect
     predictions, 0.048, against an estimated variance component of 0.094. Site
     was understated by about half, and every other block inflated against it.

What replaces it is a drop-one-block comparison: refit the model without each
block in turn and record how far deviance explained falls, in percentage points.
This is invariant to contrast coding, it is a statement about the fitted model
rather than about total ecological variation, and it can carry an interval.
It is not additive: blocks share variation, so the drops do not sum to the
model's deviance explained and should never be presented as shares of it.

deviance explained, same model without the site random effect: 48.9%

These are conditional, non-additive measures of each block's contribution to
this model. They are not shares of total ecological variation and do not sum
to anything meaningful. A block whose interval includes zero is not
distinguishable from a block that contributes nothing.

The management row is the one that repays reading carefully, and it is the
honest answer to Q2. Protection is fixed for the whole history of a site, so
in a model that already gives every site its own intercept, management and the
random effect are competing to explain the same between-site differences.
Remove management and the random effect simply takes the work over, which is
why its drop sits at or below zero. That is not evidence of no effect — Step 3
shows the Whitsunday estimate is stable across every specification — it means
deviance explained cannot separate the two, and any method that appears to
separate them is reporting an artefact of how the model was parameterised.
The last row measures management where it is identifiable: in a model with no
site random effect at all.

H4 said unexplained site-level variation would be large relative to the
measured covariates. On these numbers it is: the site random effect is the
largest single contributor, and no covariate block approaches it.
