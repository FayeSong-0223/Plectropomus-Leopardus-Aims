4. WHERE THE VARIATION SITS
======================================================================
Each block contributes a vector of values to the linear predictor. The variance
of each block is its unique contribution; the pairwise covariances are what the
blocks share. Variances and covariances together sum exactly to the variance of
the linear predictor, so nothing is allocated arbitrarily and the answer does
not depend on the order terms were entered.

term-to-block assignment:
  Management: NTR, REGION:NTR
  Habitat: s(rugosity), s(LHC), s(depth)
  Environment: s(kd490), s(maxDHW), s(Cyclone)
  Space/time: REGION, s(YEAR):REGIONPalm, s(YEAR):REGIONWhitsunday, EXPOSURE
  Site (RE): s(SITE)

7. THE THERMAL SMOOTH, INTERROGATED
======================================================================
s(maxDHW) is the strongest environmental term, but the fitted curve RISES
above about 4 degree heating weeks, which would mean heat stress increases
coral trout density. That is implausible, and the rug plot in Figure 6e shows
a gap in the data between roughly 4 and 5.8 DHW. The upper limb of the curve
therefore rests on very few region-years.
